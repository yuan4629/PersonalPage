🧭 About

I work on NLP and measurement validity in model evaluation: whether the benchmarks and automatic judges we use to rank language models are measuring what they claim to. That runs in two directions — auditing the judges themselves, and building benchmarks out of large-scale real human behavior rather than model self-play.

The same result keeps coming back across projects. A correct answer is not a correct reasoning process; persuasive language is not strategic correctness; a verdict that looks right is not one that used the right evidence. Surface competence is not underlying competence, and most of our instruments measure the first. What I want to work on for the next several years is what sits between generating language and thinking — measuring that gap, explaining it, and closing it.

🔬 Current focus → EditJudgeBias: a counterfactual audit of multimodal LLM judges
Leaderboards for image editing now rank models with MLLM judges that nobody audits. Changing only the order two candidates are shown in flips decisions at 38.2× the judge's own noise. Method, scale, findings, and what I got wrong are on that page.

🗣️ Why language

Die Grenzen meiner Sprache bedeuten die Grenzen meiner Welt.

The limits of my language mean the limits of my world.

我语言的界限意味着我的世界的界限。

Ludwig Wittgenstein, Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus 5.6

I keep that line in view because I read it as a research claim rather than an aphorism. If the limits of a language are the limits of a world, then a system whose entire access to the world runs through language inherits exactly those limits — and finding where they fall is a measurement problem, which is the kind of problem I like.

That is why my work sits in natural language processing and why I intend to stay there. Language is at once the object I study, the instrument I have to study it with, and the thing these models are most convincing at; nearly every failure I care about lives in the gap between sounding like understanding and being it.
