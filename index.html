Research

Language models are increasingly used as judges — of images, of arguments, of each other. My work asks a narrow version of that question: when a model renders a verdict, what is the verdict actually responding to?

Two threads. The first builds evaluation on large-scale real human behavior: professionally produced social-deduction gameplay, millions of human geo-guessing records — settings where humans have already supplied the ground truth and the difficulty calibration. The second goes inside the model: when a judge gets it wrong, I want a mechanistic account of how the bias formed, not just the behavioral effect.

I am currently finishing an independent first-author project on multimodal judge bias, described below.

Publications

Beyond Survival: Evaluating LLMs in Social Deduction Games with Human-Aligned Strategies

Zirui Song*, Yuan Huang*, Junchang Liu*, et al.  (* equal contribution)

EMNLP 2026 Main Co-first author arXiv:2510.11389

A benchmark built from 100+ hours of professionally produced human social-deduction gameplay — 32.4M utterance tokens, 30 roles, 15 rule variants — using winning-side MVP strategy trajectories as ground truth instead of LLM self-play. Half the SOTA models evaluated score below 0.50; the strongest reaches 0.720. The weakest dimensions are deception reasoning and counterfactual trade-off, while persuasive generation stays strong: fluency is not strategic correctness.

Geolocation with Real Human Gameplay Data: A Large-Scale Dataset and Human-Like Reasoning Framework

Zirui Song, Jingpu Yang, Yuan Huang, et al.

Preprint arXiv:2502.13759

740K users, 2.7M locations, 25M human response records, with human accuracy used to calibrate item difficulty. Multi-step geographic reasoning improves standard geolocation metrics by up to 25%; scoring the reasoning process rather than only the final answer adds a further 9%.
