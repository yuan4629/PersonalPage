🧭 About

I am a final-year undergraduate in Computer Science and Technology at Northeastern University, China, advised by Prof. Shiqi Zhao (Brain‑Computer Interface and Hardware Acceleration Lab) since 2024. I rank 5 / 100 in my cohort with a CGPA of 4.05 / 5.00 and hold the China National Scholarship. My work on evaluating LLM social reasoning against real human gameplay appears at EMNLP 2026 Main Conference, where I am a co-first author; before that I worked on geolocation from large-scale human gameplay data. Currently working on measurement validity, LLM‑ and MLLM‑as‑a‑judge auditing, multimodal evaluation, and benchmarks built from human behavior.

Underneath all of it is one question: whether the benchmarks and automatic judges we rank models with measure what they claim to. I come at it from two sides — auditing the judges themselves, and building benchmarks out of real human behavior rather than model self-play — and the same result keeps returning. A correct answer is not a correct reasoning process; persuasive language is not strategic correctness; a verdict that looks right is not one that used the right evidence. Surface competence is not underlying competence, and most of our instruments measure only the first. Measuring that gap, explaining it, and closing it is what I want to spend the next several years on.

🔬 Current focus → EditJudgeBias: a counterfactual audit of multimodal LLM judges
Leaderboards for image editing now rank models with MLLM judges that nobody audits. Changing only the order two candidates are shown in flips decisions at 38.2× the judge's own noise. Method, scale, findings, and what I got wrong are on that page.

🗣️ Why language

Die Grenzen meiner Sprache bedeuten die Grenzen meiner Welt.

The limits of my language mean the limits of my world.

我语言的界限意味着我的世界的界限。

Ludwig Wittgenstein, Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus 5.6

I keep that line in view because I read it as a research claim rather than an aphorism. If the limits of a language are the limits of a world, then a system whose entire access to the world runs through language inherits exactly those limits — and finding where they fall is a measurement problem, which is the kind of problem I like.

That is why my work sits in natural language processing and why I intend to stay there. Language is at once the object I study, the instrument I have to study it with, and the thing these models are most convincing at; nearly every failure I care about lives in the gap between sounding like understanding and being it.
